# What is Meat Protocol?

Meat Protocol is an informal name for a hand-operated transport step between systems: a person has already identified the useful information, then manually moves the information—or merely its reference—to the next system.

The “meat” is the human body doing the relay. The name is playful; the observation is about interaction design.

## Selector vs. transporter

| Role | Valuable action | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human as selector | chooses what is relevant, safe, or intended | “Compare these three files.” |
| Human as transporter | mechanically delivers the selected item | copying each file path into a chat box |

The project seeks to reduce the second role without erasing the first.

## Candidate test

Ask: **Is the person deciding something at this step, or simply carrying an already-decided payload?**

It is a Meat Protocol candidate when the step is mostly transport and a system could plausibly perform it with the right authorization, interface, and safeguards.

## What can be transported?

- content, such as a copied response;
- a reference, such as a local path, URL, or item identifier;
- state, such as an error, selection, or application context; or
- reconstructed context, such as decisions re-explained after switching sessions.

Not every screenshot, summary, or upload is a problem. They may embody useful selection, redaction, explanation, or consent. Classify the mechanical relay—not the human judgment around it.
