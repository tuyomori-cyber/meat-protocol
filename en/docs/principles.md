# Principles

## Human as selector, not transporter

Humans retain intent: choosing scope, deciding relevance, giving consent, and reviewing outcomes. Tools should take responsibility for mechanical handoffs when they can do so safely.

## Classification and automation are separate questions

Classifying a workflow as Meat Protocol does not prescribe full automation or removal of people. Decide separately which roles can, should, and must remain human.

## Separate judgment from mechanics

Selection, routing, transformation, judgment, approval, and transport often mix in one workflow. Decompose them before proposing a solution; a mechanical portion may be removable even when judgment remains valuable.

## Automation can move the boundary

Today’s necessary decision can become tomorrow’s rule, metadata, context, API, or integration. Record that transition rather than treating the human/machine boundary as fixed.

## Observe before prescribing

Make the current flow visible before recommending APIs, integrations, plugins, shared storage, or UI changes.

## Preserve history

A workaround or later integration can make a relay obsolete. Keep the entry and mark its status; Meat-to-Auto history records how interfaces changed.

## Keep the joke humane

The phrase is intentionally ridiculous. The catalog should not ridicule people, accessibility needs, or products. The joke is that capable systems can still require a person to carry a pointer across the room.
